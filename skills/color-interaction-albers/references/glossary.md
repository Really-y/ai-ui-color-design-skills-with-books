# Glossary — Albers' terms, in his sense

Alphabetical. Chapter in parentheses. Quotations are Albers'.

- **Actual fact** (XXV): what is perceived — opaque seen as transparent, 3 colors as 4, boundaries
  vibrating or vanishing. "Actual" is related to "action": changing with time. Contrast *factual
  fact*.
- **Admission tickets** (XXV): the trial studies each student must bring to the next class.
- **After-image / simultaneous contrast** (VIII): seeing the complement (e.g. green after staring
  at red) when the eye shifts to white; "the cause of most color illusions."
- **Bezold effect** (XIII): optical mixture in which changing one color alone changes the whole
  combination (Wilhelm von Bezold's rug designs).
- **Boundary** (XI, XX, XXII, XXIII): the meeting of two color areas; its softness/hardness measures
  nearness/distance; can be made to vibrate or vanish.
- **Cast / actors / performance / stage** (XVI): a fixed set of colors, each color, an arrangement,
  the unchanging format.
- **Color deception / color illusion** (Intro, IV): any case where we "see" and "read" other colors
  than those physically present.
- **Color instrumentation** (XIX): a painter's selection and presentation of color, re-performed
  in paper.
- **Color intensity (brightness)** (V, XIV): the second decisive characteristic of a color; the
  "reddest red"; rarely agreed upon in a group.
- **Color interpenetration** (Preface, XV): the middle color reading as both parents in reversed
  placement.
- **Color relatedness** (XVIII): the evaluation measure for free studies — color "for color's
  sake," autonomous, not an accompaniment to form.
- **Complementary** (XVI): "a color accompanied by its after-image"; topographically vague.
- **Contrast reversal** (VIII): a double, reversed after-image showing leftover shapes in the
  original color.
- **Factual fact** (XXV): measured, unchanging data such as wave length.
- **Film color** (XVII): "a thin, transparent, translucent layer between the eye and an object,
  independent of the object's surface color"; also the transparence effect of very small steps
  (XIV).
- **Fluting effect** (XV, XX, Preface): illusion of volume like the channeling of Doric columns,
  produced by intersecting colors or broad scale steps.
- **Free studies** (XVIII): independent work with color, mostly homework, judged by color
  relatedness.
- **Gestaltungstrieb / Beschäftigungstrieb** (XXV): the need to be productive and creative vs. the
  drive to be occupied or entertained.
- **Influencing / influenced color** (IV): the ground that changes vs. the figure that is changed.
- **Interaction / interdependence** (XXV): the mutual influencing of colors, seen from either side.
- **Intersecting colors** (XV): a constellation that lets even an untrained eye read the
  constituents of a mixture and their amounts.
- **Light intensity (lightness)** (V, XXIII, XXV): the first decisive characteristic of a color;
  Albers' replacement for the misused word "value."
- **Middle mixture** (XI, XV): a mixture equidistant in light and in hue from both parents; "the
  most significant and the most difficult"; reads frontal with equally soft/hard boundaries.
- **Mixture parents / color descendant** (IX): the two colors and the color that reads as their
  mixture.
- **Optical mixture** (XII): two or more colors perceived simultaneously merging into one new color
  (dots, halftone); depends on size and distance.
- **Optical penetration** (Preface): the middle-mixture case where either crossing color can be
  read in front — both on the same level.
- **Placement** (Intro, XI, XVI): where a color sits relative to others; "middle placement" is the
  search for the middle mixture; shifts of dominance result in shifts of placement.
- **Plastic action** (XI): the spatial organization of color read through boundaries.
- **Pronouncement** (Intro, XVI): emphasis by separating or connecting boundaries, and by quantity.
- **Psychic effect vs. physical fact** (Intro, IV, XX): the perceived vs. the measurable.
- **Psychological engineering** (XIV, XXV): a presentation so convincing it "eliminates misleading
  reading of the study's purpose and its desired effect"; the opposite is scales separated by lines.
- **Pushing / pulling** (VII, XII): colors driving each other's light and hue toward opposite
  qualities (difference) or toward similarity.
- **Quantity** (Intro, XVI): amount — extension in area ("how much") and recurrence — extension in
  number ("how often"); "quantity is a quality."
- **Recurrence** (Intro, XVI): how often a color appears; weight in time.
- **Relativity** (IV, XXV): one phenomenon having varying readings because of changing measure or
  viewpoint; "color is the most relative medium in art."
- **Reversed grounds** (VI): two grounds on which one color shows two faces echoing each other's
  ground.
- **Space-illusion** (XI): reading colors as above/below, in front/behind.
- **Step ahead** (XXV): a later problem discovered by accident in current studies and presented as
  emanating from the class.
- **Subtraction of color** (VII): "any ground subtracts its own hue [and light] from colors which it
  carries."
- **Surface color** (XVII): the color an object is named by.
- **Thinking in situations** (IV, XI, XXV): continuing comparison and observation that treats each
  color problem as a situation to be solved by trial, not a rule to be applied.
- **Topologically in the middle** (VI): the position of the only color that can echo two grounds.
- **Transformation / parallel intervals** (XIV): transposing a color constellation to a higher or
  lower key keeping its light-intensity intervals.
- **Variant vs. variety** (XXV): a thorough re-doing within a scheme vs. a discredited excuse for
  arbitrary change.
- **Vibrating boundaries / enforced contours** (XXII): doubled or glowing edges between hues that
  contrast strongly but are close in light intensity.
- **Volume color** (XVII): color existing in 3-dimensional transparent fluids, deepening with
  thickness.
- **Vanishing boundaries** (XXIII): the boundary made invisible by exact equal light intensity of
  adjacent hues.
- **Weber-Fechner law** (XX): "the visual perception of an arithmetical progression depends upon a
  physical geometric progression."
